Probed 96 inputs (60 realistic, 36 memes/noise/situations) through the shipped Rust model. By hand grading, about 45% and 58% of replies were nonsensical; 17 of 27 nonsensical realistic replies had confidence >= 0.85. Measured causes: hash collisions (459 training features in 371 of 1,024 slots; 'I don't know' is read as robots_are/feel_empty; only 43%/27% of probe features were in training, 19%/27% borrow meaning by collision, the rest are untrained random vectors averaged in), no trained 'none of these' class, missing conversational intents, 'you' scaffolding leaking into COMPUTER, saturation from overfitting, and reply rotation ignoring input. Prototyped two policy fixes: an unknown-feature gate (safe but bland, loses ~7 correct replies) and reflect-and-recall on abstention (much more ELIZA-like). Key conclusion: policy fixes change what the page says, not what the model reports; in-distribution temperature scaling will not cure this. The fix is a trained escape hatch plus UNK dropout plus no collisions. Findings in docs/experiments/SL01-dialog-probe.md; tooling in tdm-model examples and a demos/eliza/sim crate. Gate clean.
